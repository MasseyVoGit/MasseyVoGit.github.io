AI-Driven Traffic Signal Optimization

Adaptive control via Q-Learning, evaluated in SUMO across 11 connected-vehicle penetration rates

RoleIndependent Researcher
InstitutionUEC / AWCC, Tokyo
AdvisorProf. Takeo Fujii
StackPython · SUMO · Q-Learning
Year2024

Overview

This research designs and evaluates a reinforcement learning system that adapts traffic signal timing in real time. Conducted during a year-long academic exchange at the Advanced Wireless Communication Research Center (AWCC) at the University of Electro-Communications in Tokyo, the work targets a real urban mobility problem: traditional fixed-timing signals cannot respond to live traffic conditions, leading to inefficiency, idling, and avoidable congestion.

The proposed algorithm uses Q-Learning to dynamically extend or hold green signal time at an intersection based on queue length and Connected and Automated Vehicle (CAV) data. The system was simulated and benchmarked against two industry baselines — Fixed-Time (FT) control and Traffic-Actuated Control (TAC) — across CAV market penetration rates from 0% to 100%.

Approach

State representation. Twenty-four states defined by the relative ordering of queue lengths across the four approaches at the intersection, giving the algorithm a compact but expressive view of current conditions.

Action space. Two actions per decision point: extend the current green phase by one second, or hold. Small, frequent decisions let the algorithm adapt smoothly rather than make disruptive switches.

Reward function. A weighted combination of queue length measured by loop detectors and queue length detected by CAVs. The weighting shifts based on current CAV market penetration — the algorithm leans more on CAV data as more connected vehicles appear in the traffic mix.

Simulation environment. A four-leg, three-lanes-per-approach intersection built in SUMO (Simulation of Urban Mobility), with 1,000 vehicles per hour per approach. The Intelligent Driver Model (IDM) was used for human-driven vehicles and the Cooperative Adaptive Cruise Control (CACC) model for CAVs.

Results

The proposed algorithm consistently outperformed both Fixed-Time and Traffic-Actuated baselines, with the largest improvements at higher CAV penetration rates (above 50%).
